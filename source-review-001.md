# Source Review 001: Foundations for Reducing Admin Burden with Practical AI

**Status:** initial synthesis from the first evidence-register batch.  
**Related register entries:** SRC-001 to SRC-012.  
**Date:** 2026-05-11.

## Purpose

This note summarises the first source batch for the research project. It is not the full paper. It identifies early foundations, tensions, and next research gaps.

## Sources covered

The first evidence batch covers four foundation areas:

1. AI risk and governance.
2. Data protection and secure AI deployment.
3. SME digital adoption and automation barriers.
4. Service simplification and admin-burden reduction.

## Initial synthesis

### 1. The strongest governance pattern is risk-based, not tool-based

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework, OECD AI Principles, UK Government AI Playbook, ICO AI/Data Protection Toolkit, EU AI Act framing, and NCSC secure AI guidance all point in the same direction: organisations should not start with the question, "Which AI tool can we use?"

The better question is:

> What workflow, risk, data, decision, and accountability context are we putting AI into?

For non-technical teams, this means governance must be translated into plain operational rules:

- What can AI draft?
- What can AI summarise?
- What can AI classify?
- What needs human review?
- What should not be automated?
- What data should not be entered into the tool?
- Who owns the final decision?

### 2. SME adoption is constrained by execution, not just awareness

The UK SME digital adoption sources and OECD SME digital transformation work suggest that small organisations can benefit from digital tools, automation, and better information flows, but adoption is constrained by time, skills, money, confidence, and implementation capacity.

This matters because a research output aimed at SMEs cannot assume:

- technical teams;
- spare implementation capacity;
- formal governance functions;
- clean data;
- stable processes;
- willingness to tolerate long setup cycles.

The output needs to favour low-friction patterns, clear checklists, and small pilots.

### 3. Simplify before automating

The OECD administrative simplification material and GOV.UK service design standards are important because they stop the project becoming AI-first nonsense.

The working principle should be:

> Do not automate a broken process until you understand why the process creates work.

Practical implication:

- map the workflow first;
- remove duplicate steps where possible;
- clarify ownership;
- join up channels;
- define a clean output;
- only then decide whether AI or automation is useful.

### 4. Admin burden often appears as failure demand

The service design sources are especially useful because they frame complexity as something that creates extra demand: mistakes, support requests, rework, workaround channels, and trust damage.

For this research, admin burden should not only mean "time spent on admin". It should include:

- avoidable rework;
- repeated status chasing;
- inconsistent intake;
- manual reconciliation;
- duplicated capture;
- unclear handoffs;
- support demand created by confusing workflows;
- reporting drag caused by poor upstream data.

## Early research claims worth developing

These are not final conclusions yet. They are candidate claims for the paper once more evidence is added.

### Candidate claim 1

AI adoption for admin reduction should start with workflow mapping and risk classification, not tool selection.

**Current support:** SRC-001, SRC-003, SRC-004, SRC-006, SRC-010.

### Candidate claim 2

For SMEs, successful practical AI adoption depends less on advanced capability and more on low-friction implementation, trusted advice, clear ownership, and support that fits limited capacity.

**Current support:** SRC-004, SRC-005, SRC-011.

### Candidate claim 3

The safest early AI use cases are assistive rather than autonomous: drafting, summarisation, classification, routing, evidence collation, and exception surfacing with human review.

**Current support:** SRC-001, SRC-002, SRC-003, SRC-006, SRC-007, SRC-012.

### Candidate claim 4

Admin burden reduction should treat simplicity and joined-up service design as first-class interventions, not just add automation on top of existing friction.

**Current support:** SRC-008, SRC-009, SRC-010.

# Methodology

## Research question

How can non-technical organisations use practical AI and automation to reduce admin burden while preserving human judgement, accountability, and operational safety?

## Scope

This project focuses on cross-industry patterns. Industry appendices translate the core findings into implementation strategies, but the main research claim should remain general unless the evidence clearly supports a sector-specific conclusion.

Primary focus areas:

1. Reducing admin burden.
2. Small-business automation patterns.
3. AI governance for non-technical teams.

## Source selection criteria

Prefer sources that are:

- Publicly accessible or clearly referenceable.
- Recent enough for the claim being made.
- From credible institutions, regulators, researchers, public bodies, standards organisations, or experienced operators.
- Directly relevant to admin burden, automation, AI governance, process improvement, organisational design, or digital transformation.

Use caution with:

- Vendor marketing material.
- Unsourced opinion pieces.
- Outdated AI adoption claims.
- Sector-specific claims being generalised without evidence.
- Productivity claims without measurement detail.

## Evidence register rules

Every material source should be entered into `evidence-register.csv`.

Each entry should capture:

- Source identity.
- Source type.
- Publisher.
- Year.
- URL.
- Topic area.
- Key claim.
- Evidence strength.
- Limitations.
- Whether it is used in the output.
- Notes on interpretation.

## Evidence strength scale

Use a simple qualitative scale:

| Strength | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High | Strongly relevant source with clear evidence, method, or authority. |
| Medium | Useful source, but limited by scope, method, age, or generalisability. |
| Low | Contextual or illustrative only. Do not use as primary support for major claims. |
| Exclude | Not suitable for use beyond background awareness. |

## AI-assisted workflow rules

AI may be used to:

- Identify candidate sources.
- Summarise source material.
- Extract claims into structured tables.
- Cluster themes.
- Draft outlines.
- Generate template variants.
- Critique drafts for clarity, unsupported claims, and missing limitations.

AI must not be used to:

- Invent sources.
- Treat uncited claims as evidence.
- Make medical, legal, regulatory, clinical, or financial recommendations.
- Replace human review of final claims.
- Hide uncertainty.

## Claim discipline

Each recommendation should be classified as one of:

- **Evidence-backed** — directly supported by cited sources.
- **Method-derived** — follows from the stated method and evidence synthesis.
- **Practitioner judgement** — a reasoned interpretation that must be marked as such.
- **Hypothesis** — plausible but not yet tested or evidenced strongly enough.

Do not let confident wording outrun the evidence. That is how research turns into a LinkedIn ghost wearing a blazer.

## Human review expectations

Before publishing a research pack:

1. Check that all material claims link back to the evidence register.
2. Mark limitations clearly.
3. Separate general findings from industry-specific implementation notes.
4. Review any high-risk sector examples, especially healthcare, veterinary, public services, legal, finance, and regulated operations.
5. Remove or soften claims that cannot be supported.

## Recommendation grading

Recommendations should be graded by:

- Potential usefulness.
- Implementation effort.
- Risk level.
- Evidence strength.
- Need for specialist review.
- Suitability for non-technical teams.

## Publication standard

A publishable output should include:

- A clear executive summary.
- Source-backed findings.
- Practical templates or checklists.
- Limitations.
- Version date.
- Evidence register link.
- Human-review notes where relevant.
